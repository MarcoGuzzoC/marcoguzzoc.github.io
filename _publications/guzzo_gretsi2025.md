For downlink multi-user massive MIMO systems, we provide an upper bound and an analytical expression of the peak to average power ratio in the case of Rayleigh channel. In contrast to what was assumed and intuited in the literature, we examine a variety of linear precoders and explain how the Peak-to-Average-Power-Ratio (PAPR) is independent of the base station's antenna count when we have perfect channel state information

We have proposed a new analytical bound on the peak power and PAPR of a linearly precoded single-carrier signal. Although this bound is not tight for a large number of users, it clearly highlights the trend and the lack of dependence on \\(\gamma\\) once \\(\gamma \\) exceeds 5. In particular, we have proven that, contrary to the intuition derived from the state of the art, the PAPR is bounded and remains nearly constant as the number of antennas increases. Our new bound also shows that the effects of propagation fading or shadowing have no impact on the PAPR.
